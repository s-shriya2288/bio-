Protein Structure Unfolded

Scroll down to see the four distinct levels of protein architecture come to life. From a simple chain to a complex biological machine.

↓

1. Primary Structure

The foundation of a protein is its sequence of amino acids, connected by peptide bonds. Think of it like a specific sequence of glowing beads on a string. The order determines everything that follows.

2. Secondary Structure

Hydrogen bonds begin to form between the amino acids, causing the linear chain to twist and fold locally. The most common shapes are the twisting Alpha Helix (shown below) and the flat Beta-Pleated Sheet.

3. Tertiary Structure

The helices and sheets fold in on themselves to create a highly specific, three-dimensional shape. This complex folding is driven by side-chain interactions, creating a functional, dynamic "blob".

4. Quaternary Structure

Many proteins aren't finished yet! They consist of multiple folded polypeptide chains (subunits) that lock together to form one massive, functional complex, like hemoglobin or DNA polymerase.
